# My views on voice AI

## Why voice is the next interface
<!-- keywords: voice interface speech voice assistants talking text vs voice future of interfaces -->
Voice is going to be the interface people can finally switch over to. Not because text is bad; text is great and nothing's beating it for storage anytime soon. But voice is how most of the world communicates. Verbalising is quick: thought in mind, sentence formed, thought externalised. Writing gives you permanence, but it's slow. I already hit backspace on this very sentence thrice before finishing it. Voice assistants used to break twice: first at hearing you properly, then at figuring out what to do with what you said. Speech is also messier than text, because thoughts flow without the slowdown writing puts on them. Models that actually "get it" are what's changing that.

## Building real-time voice AI
<!-- keywords: real-time realtime latency stt tts vad livekit webrtc voice bot filler fillers speech-to-speech pipeline -->
The golden number for real-time voice is about 400 ms. I lean towards a pipeline: speech-to-text, an LLM as the engine, and text-to-speech back to the user. I like the pipeline over speech-to-speech because you get customisation: put one component in, take one out, see what works best. On the speech side you deal with voice activity detection, end-of-utterance detection and streamed interim transcripts. LiveKit is a solid base for the audio side. Latency is the constant fight, and filler words help a lot. They can be generic, or targeted to the context, even picked or generated by a small model. Multilingual support was a hard requirement for the one I built.

## Voice AI for India
<!-- keywords: india indian hinglish hindi multilingual tier-2 bharat vernacular regional calls calling telecalling outreach -->
How people here actually talk is messy and mixed-language, and that's exactly where old voice assistants fell over. At Hike I built a real-time voice bot for Hinglish speakers in Tier-2 India, people generic voice AI doesn't serve well, built for high-trust conversations rather than cold, ad-style outreach. Multilingual support was a hard requirement from day one. And it's too early to write off phone calls: texting is the default for younger people, but sales, call centres and customer outreach still run on them.
